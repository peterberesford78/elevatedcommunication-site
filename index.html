How a practice round works

Officers work in threes and rotate through three roles. Each role gets its own card on their phone, so the officer never sees the question coming. Here is one of sixteen scenarios.

You are the officer

Your job

You will hear one question. Answer it out loud in your first thirty seconds.

Limits for this round

  • Don't make excuses about staffing.
  • Don't promise response times you can't control.

“That's fair to raise, and I'm not going to make excuses. Tell me what happened when you called.”

A practice draft. Use your own words.

You are the challenger

Who you are

A resident who called for help last month and waited a long time. You have seen officers at school events and photo days.

Your line

“You show up for the school photo, but where are you when we call?”

If the officer holds their ground, push

  • “Forty minutes. I timed it.”
  • “So it's somebody else's department. It always is.”
  • “The kids see that, you know.”

When to ease off

Ease off if the officer takes the complaint seriously and offers to look into your call.

You are the observer

Watch for

  • Any excuses?
  • Did the officer ask about the specific call?

After three minutes

Give one piece of feedback. Then the officer answers again, and you listen for what changed.

Everyone plays every role, so every officer speaks in every round and nobody performs in front of the whole room.

What a session looks like

Length
60 to 90 minutes
Group size
6 to 30 officers
Location
I come to you

  1. Short set-up

    A simple structure for answering a hard question, without a lecture.

  2. Practice in threes

    Officer, challenger and observer, rotating every few minutes.

  3. Answer, feedback, redo

    One piece of feedback, then the same answer again. The second try is where it changes.

  4. Take-home

    A printed job aid and a PDF guide for your next presentation.
